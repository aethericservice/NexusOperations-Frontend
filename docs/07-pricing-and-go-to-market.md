# 07. Pricing and Go-to-Market

## Core Pricing Model

Use a SaaS pricing model that reflects both platform access and usage value.

### Suggested pricing structure

#### Starter
- $49–$99/month
- small businesses
- 1–3 integrations
- limited alert volume
- standard dashboard

#### Growth
- $199–$499/month
- growing companies
- more integrations
- workflow automation included
- more AI usage
- team access

#### Enterprise
- $1,000+/month
- custom requirements
- advanced security
- priority support
- custom integrations
- SLA and implementation support

## Usage-Based Add-ons

Consider additional billing for:

- AI requests or tokens
- workflow executions
- extra integrations
- premium support
- custom onboarding
- connector implementation

## Go-to-Market Strategy

### 1. Start with a narrow niche
Pick one customer profile and sell the pain clearly.

Examples:
- service businesses
- e-commerce brands
- agencies

### 2. Sell ROI, not AI wording
Lead with business outcomes and operational value.

Examples:
- reduce missed jobs
- reduce customer churn risk
- detect service issues faster
- save staff time
- prevent revenue loss

### 3. Offer a pilot
A pilot is useful for proving value before long-term contract.

Pilot could include:
- 2–4 integrations
- 30-day trial
- alert dashboard
- AI summary for business owner
- review and recommendations

### 4. Build customer proof
Capture use cases and ROI metrics from early users.

Examples:
- 32% reduction in unresolved issues
- 18% faster response time
- 22% fewer missed service events
- saved manager time per week

### 5. Expand via connectors and use cases
Once a customer is onboarded, focus on add-on value via:

- more integrations
- workflow templates
- new alerts
- premium support

## Sales Angle

A strong sales sentence:

> We help businesses see operational problems across their connected tools before they affect customers, revenue, or team performance.

## Ideal Customer Profile

A good early customer has:

- multiple internal tools
- recurring operational problems
- high value per customer issue
- enough business scale to justify SaaS spend
- willingness to integrate systems

## Channel Strategy

Best early channels:
- direct founder-led sales
- outreach to local business owners
- niche communities
- Facebook groups or industry communities
- LinkedIn outreach
- partnerships with agencies or consultants

## Conversion Strategy

Focus sales conversations on:

- current pain and data fragmentation
- visible cost of operating without insight
- how quickly the system can surface problems
- what actions and alerts are most valuable

## Long-Term Growth Strategy

Once the MVP has success in one segment:

1. add adjacent customer verticals
2. expand connectors
3. standardize onboarding
4. create reusable workflow templates
5. grow to multi-product SaaS offer

## Final Recommendation

The winning startup strategy is not to sell “AI for business.” It is to sell a focused operational risk layer for a clear customer segment with specific business pain and measurable ROI.
